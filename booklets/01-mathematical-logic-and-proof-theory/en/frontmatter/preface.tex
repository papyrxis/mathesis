This is where \textit{Mathesis} starts, because it's where I kept
getting stuck. I could follow a proof line by line and still not be able
to tell you why it was a proof and not just a sequence of true-sounding
sentences. So this booklet starts before any specific branch of
mathematics: propositional and predicate logic, the standard proof
techniques, natural deduction, the Curry-Howard correspondence between
proofs and programs, and a first look at constructive mathematics.

Everything else in \textit{Mathesis} leans on this booklet without
saying so out loud. That's also why it's short: the goal isn't to cover
logic exhaustively, it's to cover enough of it that ``why is this a
valid proof'' stops being a question I have to guess the answer to.
