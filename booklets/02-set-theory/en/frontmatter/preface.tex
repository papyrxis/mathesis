Once logic is in place, the next question is what mathematics is
actually built \textit{out of}. The answer this booklet works through is:
sets, and not much else. Naive set theory, its paradoxes, the ZFC axioms
that patch them, relations and functions, ordinals, cardinality, the
Axiom of Choice, and a bridge to type theory.

This one leans directly on Booklet~01 --- the paradoxes here are exactly
the kind of thing the previous booklet's proof techniques exist to
handle carefully. Almost every later booklet leans on this one in turn;
``let $X$ be a set'' is a sentence the rest of \textit{Mathesis} uses
constantly and rarely re-justifies.
