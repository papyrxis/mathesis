Abstract algebra asks a blunter question than linear algebra
does: forget what the elements \textit{are} --- numbers, symmetries,
matrices --- and ask what's true just because of how they combine. This
booklet works through groups, subgroups and homomorphisms, group
actions, rings and ideals, fields and Galois theory, Boolean algebra,
and lattices.

Galois theory is the payoff chapter: it answers, using group theory
alone, why some equations that look perfectly reasonable have no
algebraic solution. That's the kind of result that made we want to
write this booklet in the first place --- structure explaining
something computation alone can't.
