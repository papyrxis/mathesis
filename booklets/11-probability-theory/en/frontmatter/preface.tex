Probability theory takes uncertainty and gives it the same
rigor Booklet~07 gave to limits --- which turns out to mean building it
on measure theory rather than on intuition about coin flips. This
booklet covers probability spaces and $\sigma$-algebras, random
variables, expectation and variance, concentration inequalities, the
law of large numbers and the central limit theorem, Markov chains,
martingales, Brownian motion, information theory and entropy, and the
probabilistic method.

It depends directly on Booklet~07's measure theory and Booklet~02's set
theory. Information theory and entropy are the chapters I'm most
looking forward to actually writing --- they're where probability
starts explaining things about computation and communication, not just
about randomness.
